DiRAC’s GPU Performance Portability Training project (GPPT) is designed to support researchers in developing and optimising codes on GPU hardware through comprehensive, practical training.

As the UK’s physics community wrestles with the rise of GPUs in HPC, skills and training in the effective use of these accelerators becomes crucial to sustain a healthy community. The additional complexity demanded of scientific codes to target new and powerful hardware architectures also demands more careful software design. Through a holistic approach that focuses on the many aspects of GPU-accelerated software development, this project teaches researchers the techniques and skills to write not just highly performant GPU code, but high-quality, portable code.

By engaging with these resources you will be able to:

  • Understand the hardware and software landscapes of GPU programming as of 2026
  • Make informed decisions about technology choice and software design
  • Profile and optimise GPU codes

Course 1: GPU Programming Principles

You’ve attended an introductory course on CUDA, you’ve dabbled in OpenMP offloading, and you’ve asked ChatGPT to help you optimise your HIP code; but now you’re wondering why your code is more complicated and still slower than you’d like. This intermediate-level course offers an overview of the key techniques in architecture, software design, performance profiling and optimisation, that will help guide the development of high-quality, performant, portable GPU codes.

Course 2: Performance Optimisation and Profiling

Following on from GPU Programming Principles, this course delves into the world of performance profiling and optimisation. We use Nvidia’s profiling tools to showcase a variety of common bottlenecks, how to identify them with data from the profilers, and possible optimisations.

Deep Dives

Our deep dives feature experts from a variety of disciplines giving overviews of their production scientific codes that have already been ported to GPUs. Their experience can inform your own development strategy and technology choice. You can learn how others have handled the tradeoffs that must be made in the porting process, and the design decisions required to keep a multi-architecture program maintainable and performant.

Workshop 1: 15-17 Sept 2026

REGISTRATION NOW CLOSED.

If you’re a computational researcher within the STFC, IRIS, or Ada Lovelace Centre communities and are thinking about adapting your codebase for GPUs, this workshop is designed for you.

Presentation sessions will introduce GPU hardware and survey the current software landscape, before covering adapting algorithms for greater GPU-effectiveness, and an introduction to software architecture. The day is completed with a focussed ”Deep Dive” led by an experienced practitioner with real-world insights from porting their own codes, and a supervised exercise in porting code from CPUs to GPUs using a selection of programming languages.

Activities shift further towards hands-on learning, with continued mentor-led exercises and further “Deep Dive” sessions. Further presentations will cover software engineering for multi-architecture software, performance monitoring and profiling, and both single-GPU and multi-GPU optimisation, as well as a review of other available GPU training packages. You’ll also have access to drop-in code clinics, where Research Software Engineers will offer tailored advice and guidance, whether you’re interested in refining existing code or planning the first steps towards GPU migration for your project

DiRAC's GPU Performance Portability Training project (GPPT) is designed to support researchers in developing and porting codes on GPU hardware through comprehensive, practical training.

As the UK's physics community wrestles with the rise of GPUs in HPC, skills and training in the effective use of these accelerators becomes crucial to sustain a healthy community. The additional complexity demanded of scientific codes to target new and powerful hardware architectures also demands more careful software design. Through a holistic approach that focuses on the many aspects of good GPU-accelerated software development, this project teaches researchers the techniques and skills to write not just highly performant GPU code, but high-quality, portable code.

The project is made up of two self-paced courses, two in-person workshops, and a collection of deep dives into existing ported codes, showcasing success stories from our community.

## Course 1: GPU Programming Principles

You've attended an introductory course on CUDA, you've dabbled in OpenMP offloading, and you've asked ChatGPT to help you optimise your HIP code; but now you're wondering why your code is more complicated and still slower than you'd like. This intermediate-level course offers an overview of the key techniques in architecture, software design, performance profiling and optimisation, that will help guide the development of high-quality, performant, portable GPU codes.

## Course 2: Performance Optimisation and Profiling

Following on from GPU Programming Principles, this course delves into the world of performance profiling and optimisation. We use Nvidia's profiling tools to showcase a variety of common bottlenecks, how to identify them with data from the profilers, and possible optimisations.
